Philosophy

Most outreach is noise.
Mine is a reason to reply.

I built my process by failing first. Early on, I sent emails that got ignored, and I paid attention to why. Over time, I figured out how to make each element (the research, the angle, the opener) feel like a genuine conversation with a peer rather than a pitch landing in someone's inbox.

Three years in, the core logic hasn't changed: approach prospects with something that's actually useful to them. When the deal creates more value for their side than it asks for, the reply almost writes itself. Getting them to see it that way is the job.

  • Precision targeting, when it matters: If a client needs a link from a specific site, a particular page, a brand mention, or a listicle slot, I'll track it down. Insertion, mention, roundup, whatever the format calls for. I've learned how to hunt for very specific placements without burning the relationship in the process.
  • Scale, when the brief calls for it: High-volume campaigns need a different approach. I've built templated outreach that reads like it was written one-to-one, so a blast to hundreds of recipients doesn't feel like one.

My Psychology of Successful Campaigns

High reply rates are not luck. They are the output of a repeatable system that I adapt based on what the campaign actually needs, whether that's surgical precision or efficient scale.

1

Keyword & Competitor Intel

Before prospecting starts, I map every linkable asset and identify who is already linking to competing content. This tells me which doors are open before I knock, and which angles are most likely to land.

1
2
2

Prospecting That Fits the Brief

For targeted campaigns, strict DR, traffic, and topical relevancy filters narrow the list to sites that will actually move the needle. For high-volume campaigns, I use automation tools to build and segment prospect lists quickly, without compromising on relevancy. No PBNs. No link farms.

3

Personalization at the Right Level

For specific, high-value targets, I dig into the site and write openers that feel genuinely tailored. For scale campaigns, I use AI-assisted tools to build templates that read as personal without requiring manual effort on every send. The approach matches the output required.

3
4
4

The Right Angle, Every Time

Value swap, broken-link fix, expert quote, listicle pitch. I match the outreach angle to the prospect type, so the ask feels like a logical next step rather than a cold interruption.

5

Smart Send & Follow-Up

Sequences go out with proper send-time logic and domain warming in place. Follow-ups add something new to the thread rather than just nudging. The goal is replies, not blocklists.

Relationship Capital

Every editor who says yes gets logged for the long term. A first placement is an opening. The compounding value comes from what happens after.
